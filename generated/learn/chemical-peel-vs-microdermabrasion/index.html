Chemical peel vs microdermabrasion

Chemical peels and microdermabrasion both exfoliate the skin to improve tone and texture, but they do it in completely different ways. Microdermabrasion physically buffs away the surface with no downtime; a chemical peel uses acids to dissolve dead skin and can reach deeper. The right choice depends on your concern and how much recovery time you can spare.

How each treatment works

Microdermabrasion is mechanical exfoliation — a diamond-tip or crystal wand sands off the outermost layer of the epidermis. A chemical peel is chemical exfoliation: a provider applies an acid solution — glycolic, lactic or salicylic for light peels, TCA for medium ones — that triggers a controlled shedding of skin layers. Microdermabrasion stays strictly superficial, while peels can be dialed from light to deep.

Which concern each one fits

Microdermabrasion suits mild, surface-level goals: dullness, rough texture, enlarged pores and a quick pre-event refresh, and it is gentle enough for sensitive skin that reacts poorly to acids. Chemical peels reach further, making them the stronger choice for stubborn hyperpigmentation, sun damage, melasma, post-acne discoloration and fine lines. For pitted acne scars or laxity, a collagen-building option like microneedling often beats both.

Downtime and frequency

Microdermabrasion has essentially no downtime — minor redness at most — and is typically repeated every two to four weeks. Light peels also involve little to no visible peeling and can be done roughly monthly, but medium and deep peels bring several days to a couple of weeks of flaking and redness and are spaced much further apart. Sun protection matters after either treatment, and especially after a peel.

How to choose

If you want a gentle, no-downtime glow and your concerns are mild, microdermabrasion is a reasonable start. If you are targeting pigmentation, sun damage or fine lines and can tolerate some peeling, a chemical peel usually delivers more. Many providers alternate the two across a treatment plan — a licensed provider can tell you which fits your skin.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better for hyperpigmentation, a chemical peel or microdermabrasion?

A chemical peel is generally more effective for stubborn pigmentation, melasma and sun spots because it works deeper than microdermabrasion, which mainly refreshes the surface.

Is microdermabrasion or a chemical peel gentler?

Microdermabrasion is the gentler of the two, with no acids and essentially no downtime, which makes it a common choice for sensitive skin or a first treatment.

Can you do microdermabrasion and a chemical peel together?

Some providers combine or alternate them across a treatment plan, since they exfoliate differently. The sequencing should come from a licensed provider rather than doing both on your own.
