How much does IV therapy cost in Florida?

IV therapy is priced per session, and how much you pay depends mostly on the formula in the bag. In Florida, published prices most commonly run about $100 to $400 per session — basic hydration and simple vitamin drips at the lower end, specialty infusions like NAD+ higher. Memberships and packages typically lower the per-session cost.

How IV therapy pricing works

IV drips are billed per session, and the formula drives the price. A basic hydration or electrolyte drip is the least expensive; classic vitamin blends such as a Myers' cocktail sit in the middle; and specialty or high-dose infusions — NAD+, high-dose vitamin C, glutathione add-ons — cost the most. Many IV lounges and med spas also sell add-on boosters (extra vitamins, or anti-nausea and anti-inflammatory medications) that raise the total.

What changes your price

Beyond the formula, price varies with the provider (a physician-supervised clinic may charge more than a walk-in lounge), your location (metro areas like Miami tend to run higher), and whether the service is in-clinic or mobile — at-home mobile IV visits usually add a travel or convenience fee. Memberships, first-visit specials and multi-session packages are the main ways to bring the per-drip cost down.

Getting an accurate quote

IV therapy is a cash-pay wellness service, so insurance does not cover it. Reputable Florida providers list or quote a per-session price and explain what is in each drip. If you plan to go regularly, compare membership and package pricing rather than single-session rates, and confirm the clinic is staffed by licensed medical professionals. Frequently asked questions

How much is a basic IV drip in Florida?

A basic hydration or simple vitamin drip usually starts near the lower end of the roughly $100 to $400 range at Florida med spas and IV lounges. Specialty infusions such as NAD+ cost more.

Does insurance cover IV therapy?

No — elective IV vitamin and hydration therapy is a cash-pay wellness service and is not covered by insurance. Clinics often offer memberships or packages to reduce the per-session cost.

Why do IV therapy prices vary so much?

The formula is the biggest factor — a simple hydration bag costs far less than a high-dose or specialty infusion. Provider type, location, add-on boosters and whether the drip is mobile or in-clinic also change the price.
