How often should you get a HydraFacial?

For most people, a HydraFacial every 4 to 6 weeks is the sweet spot — it lines up with the skin's roughly 28-day cell turnover cycle. Some skin concerns benefit from a shorter interval at first, but going more often than every 2 weeks is generally not recommended.

The standard schedule

Skin renews itself on an approximately 28-day cycle, which is why most providers recommend a HydraFacial about once a month, or every 4 to 6 weeks. On that cadence each session maintains the exfoliation, hydration and glow of the last one instead of starting from scratch.

When a shorter interval helps

For active breakouts or very oily skin, providers often start with a session every 2 to 3 weeks for the first couple of months to clear congestion, then stretch back to monthly maintenance. For hyperpigmentation or fine-line goals, every 3 to 4 weeks is a common starting rhythm. Your provider should tailor the plan to your skin rather than selling a fixed package.

Can you get one too often?

Yes. Because each session includes physical and chemical exfoliation, going more often than every 2 weeks risks over-exfoliating and irritating the skin barrier. More is not better here — consistency is what compounds.

If you only go occasionally

A single HydraFacial before an event still delivers a same-day glow that typically lasts a few weeks. If you go quarterly, expect a fresh reset each time rather than the cumulative improvement a monthly schedule builds.

Frequently asked questions

How long do HydraFacial results last?

The visible glow and hydration from one session typically last around 4 to 6 weeks, which is exactly why monthly treatment is the standard recommendation.

Can I get a HydraFacial every 2 weeks?

Every 2 to 3 weeks is sometimes recommended short-term for acne-prone or congested skin, but more frequently than every 2 weeks risks over-exfoliation.

Is a monthly HydraFacial worth it?

If dullness, congestion or dryness are recurring concerns, monthly sessions maintain results continuously — and many med spas offer memberships that cut the per-session price.
