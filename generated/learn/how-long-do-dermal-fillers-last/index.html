How long do dermal fillers last?

Most dermal fillers last somewhere between 6 and 18 months, but the real answer depends on the product used, where it is placed, and your own metabolism. Thin fillers in high-movement areas fade faster; firmer fillers in stable areas last longer.

Typical timelines by product

Hyaluronic-acid fillers — the most common type — generally last 6 to 18 months. Lighter formulas used for lips or fine lines tend toward the shorter end, while firmer formulas built for cheeks and jawline hold longer. Calcium-hydroxylapatite fillers such as Radiesse often last around a year or more, and poly-L-lactic-acid (Sculptra), which stimulates your own collagen, can last two years or more as its effect builds gradually.

Why placement and movement matter

Filler breaks down faster in areas that move a lot. Lips, for example, are constantly in motion and metabolize filler quicker than the cheeks, which are relatively stable. Your metabolism, activity level and how much product was placed all play a role too — a fuller correction generally outlasts a light, conservative one.

Making results last

There is no need to wait until filler fully disappears; many injectors recommend a smaller touch-up before it is completely gone to keep results consistent. Staying hydrated, protecting your skin from sun, and following your provider's aftercare help. Your injector can give a realistic maintenance schedule for the specific product and area you had treated.

Frequently asked questions

Which dermal fillers last the longest?

Biostimulatory fillers like Sculptra (poly-L-lactic acid) and Radiesse (calcium hydroxylapatite) tend to last the longest — often a year to two or more — while hyaluronic-acid lip fillers are usually the shortest-lasting.

Do lip fillers last as long as cheek fillers?

Usually not. Lips move constantly and metabolize filler faster, so lip filler often needs touch-ups sooner than filler placed in more stable areas like the cheeks or jawline.

Can dermal fillers be dissolved?

Hyaluronic-acid fillers can be dissolved with an enzyme called hyaluronidase if you dislike the result or have a complication. Non-HA fillers like Radiesse and Sculptra cannot be dissolved this way, which is one reason product choice matters.
