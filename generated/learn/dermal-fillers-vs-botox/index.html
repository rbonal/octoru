Dermal fillers vs Botox

Dermal fillers and Botox are both injectables, but they do opposite jobs. Botox relaxes the muscles that cause expression lines; dermal fillers add volume to smooth folds and restore fullness. Many people use one, and plenty use both — the right choice depends on what you are trying to fix.

How each one works

Botox is a neuromodulator: it temporarily relaxes the muscle under a wrinkle so the skin above it smooths out. Dermal fillers are gels — usually hyaluronic acid — injected to add volume beneath the skin. In short, Botox softens movement, fillers restore or add structure. They are not interchangeable, which is why injectors often use them in different areas of the same face.

Which concern each one fits

Botox is best for dynamic wrinkles caused by movement: forehead lines, frown lines between the brows, and crow's feet. Dermal fillers are best for static concerns and lost volume: flattened cheeks, a soft jawline, nasolabial folds, thin lips, and under-eye hollows. If a line is there when your face is completely still, filler is usually the tool; if it appears only when you move, Botox usually is.

Cost, results and how long they last

Botox is priced per unit and typically lasts about 3 to 4 months. Fillers are priced per syringe, cost more up front, and last longer — roughly 6 to 18 months depending on the product and placement. Botox takes a few days to show; filler results are largely immediate. Because they solve different problems, comparing them on price alone misses the point — the better question is which one addresses your concern.

Can you get both?

Yes, and many people do — a common combination is Botox for the upper face and filler for the cheeks, lips or jawline. A qualified injector can map which areas call for which, and sometimes stages them across visits. The decision should come from a licensed provider after assessing your face at rest and in motion.

Frequently asked questions

Is Botox or filler better for forehead lines?

Botox is usually the better choice for forehead lines, because those are dynamic wrinkles caused by muscle movement, which Botox relaxes. Filler is aimed at volume loss and static folds instead.

Do dermal fillers last longer than Botox?

Yes. Botox typically lasts about 3 to 4 months, while most dermal fillers last from 6 to 18 months depending on the product and where it is placed.

Can you get Botox and fillers at the same time?

Often, yes — many injectors combine them in one visit, treating different areas. Whether to do both at once should be decided by a licensed provider based on your goals.
